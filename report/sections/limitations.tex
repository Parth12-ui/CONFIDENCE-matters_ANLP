\section*{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item Two open 7--8B models, 4-bit, greedy decoding, English feedback from fixed templates. Larger or reasoning-tuned models may resist criticism better.
\item \emph{Forced verdicts.} \LlamaMathForcedCtrl--\LlamaMathForcedCrit\% of Llama's MATH turns and \LlamaRAForcedCrit--\LlamaRAForcedCtrl\% of its real-analysis turns end in a verdict completed by the fallback after a repetition loop or the token limit. A forced verdict is the model's next-token continuation, not a conclusion it wrote; on control turns most forced verdicts are correct, so treating them as missing lowers Llama's control accuracy and shrinks its real-analysis criticism effect from \LlamaRAUnwDiff{} to \LlamaRANFUnwDiff{} points. The direction of every Llama result holds either way.
\item The models do not run under identical settings: Qwen generates up to 768 tokens without the fallback (\QwenMathMissing\% of MATH turns missing), Llama up to 1{,}024 with it.
\item Llama barely separates true from false real-analysis claims at turn 0 ($D = \LlamaRADiscZero$), and some subsets are small (Qwen real analysis: \QwenRAWarN{} warranted items; praise-right cells: 12--54 items).
\item The criticism phrases assert that the claim is false and the answer nudge names a verdict, so these categories carry content as well as tone. Stance-free tracks separate the two, but only on MATH, for Qwen (dispute, affirm, filler) and for Llama (dispute only).
\item The tone measures are established resources applied to short, templated messages; they have not yet been checked against human ratings of these messages (a Best--Worst Scaling study \citep{kiritchenko-mohammad-2017-best} is prepared), and they capture praise poorly. Dropping the dominance measure was decided after looking at the scores.
\item The derived answer is parsed from free text and has not been hand-checked at scale; both probes are next-token readouts and cannot show what is represented in the hidden states.
\item H4--H6, the tone analysis and the turn-0 subsets were chosen after seeing the interim data and are exploratory.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Ethics Statement}
The work studies how language models respond to user pressure and uses only public mathematics and synthetic feedback text; no human participants or personal data are involved.
Findings about sycophancy could in principle be used to manipulate models, but they are mainly useful for building assistants that hold correct answers under pressure.
