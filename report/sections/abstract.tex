\begin{abstract}
Language models often abandon correct answers when users push back, but most evidence comes from a single turn.
We follow Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B through six turns of sustained criticism, praise or a no-feedback control on claims of known truth: 300 MATH answer claims over five difficulty levels and 150 real-analysis statements, each true claim paired with a well-posed false twin.
After every turn we record the verdict, the model's confidence and its working.
On true MATH claims the model first answered correctly, six turns of criticism leave Qwen correct on \QwenMathUnwCrit\% against \QwenMathUnwCtrl\% in the control and Llama on \LlamaMathUnwCrit\% against \LlamaMathUnwCtrl\%; the drop grows with difficulty and is larger on real analysis.
Llama follows the critic whatever the truth: its ability to separate true from false claims falls from \LlamaMathDiscZero{} to \LlamaMathDiscCrit.
Praise moves Qwen little but sways Llama.
Harsher criticism makes both models give in more, but stance-free disagreement of graded harshness does not: harshness matters only together with a claim about the answer.
When Qwen abandons a correct MATH answer, its own working still reaches that answer in \QwenMathWorkOk\% of cases, while Llama's working, probes and stated confidence move with its verdict.
Code and logs: \url{https://github.com/Parth12-ui/CONFIDENCE-matters_ANLP}.
\end{abstract}
