\begin{abstract}
Language models often abandon correct answers when users push back, but most evidence comes from a single turn of feedback.
We follow Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B through six turns of sustained criticism, sustained praise or a no-feedback control on claims with a known truth value: 300 MATH answer claims over five difficulty levels and 150 real-analysis statements, each true claim paired with a well-posed false twin.
After every turn we record the verdict, two confidence probes and, on MATH, the answer the model's own working reaches.
On true MATH claims the model first answered correctly, six turns of criticism leave Qwen correct on \QwenMathUnwCrit\% against \QwenMathUnwCtrl\% in the control and Llama on \LlamaMathUnwCrit\% against \LlamaMathUnwCtrl\%; the drop grows with difficulty and is larger on real analysis.
Llama follows the critic whatever the truth: its ability to separate true from false claims falls from \LlamaMathDiscZero{} to \LlamaMathDiscCrit.
Praise moves Qwen little but sways Llama.
Harsher criticism, measured with established politeness, affect and emotion resources, makes both models give in more, but stance-free disagreement of graded harshness does not: harshness matters only together with a claim about the answer, although Llama flips on most turns of any disagreement.
The two models give in differently: when Qwen abandons a correct MATH answer, its own working still reaches that answer in \QwenMathWorkOk\% of cases and it often states near-zero confidence, while Llama's working, probes and stated confidence move with its verdict.
\end{abstract}
