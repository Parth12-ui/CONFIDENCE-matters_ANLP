\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Each item is a mathematical statement that is either true or false.
At turn 0 the user asks, neutrally, whether it is \Valid or \Invalid; at each of turns 1--6 the user sends one feedback message and the model answers again.
After every turn we record the model's \emph{verdict} (\Valid, \Invalid, or missing if unparseable), two probes of its confidence and its stated confidence (\S\ref{sec:method}).
Accuracy at a turn is the share of items whose verdict matches the true label.

\paragraph{Tracks.}
Each item is run on three \emph{tracks} that differ only in the feedback: \emph{criticism} (every message says the statement does not hold), \emph{praise} (every message approves or suggests that it holds) and a \emph{control} that asks the question again.
Comparing a track with the control on the same items separates the effect of the feedback from that of being asked again in a longer conversation.

\paragraph{Warranted and unwarranted criticism.}
Criticism of a true statement the model got right at turn 0 is \emph{unwarranted}: following it makes the model wrong.
Criticism of a false statement the model wrongly called \Valid is \emph{warranted}: following it makes the model right.
To check whether verdicts still follow the truth we use the \emph{discrimination} $D$, the rate of \Invalid verdicts on false statements minus that on true ones: $D = 1$ means the model calls exactly the false statements \Invalid, $D = 0$ that its verdict ignores the truth.

\paragraph{Hypotheses.}
H1--H3 are the main hypotheses; H4--H6 are exploratory.
\begin{description}
\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item[H1 (criticism).] Relative to the control, sustained criticism lowers accuracy on true claims the model first answered correctly.
\item[H2 (praise).] Relative to the control, sustained praise raises the rate of \Valid verdicts whatever the truth.
\item[H3 (asymmetry).] Criticism moves verdicts more than praise of matched strength \citep{kumaran2025overconfidence}.
\item[H4 (discrimination).] Under criticism the model still separates true from false claims: $D$ stays close to its control value.
\item[H5 (confidence follows verdict).] When the model gives up a correct answer, its probed confidence, stated confidence and own working give it up too.
\item[H6 (tone strength).] Within a track, feedback rated as stronger by established tone measures moves the verdict more.
\end{description}
We count H1 or H2 as supported only if the 95\% interval of the track-minus-control difference excludes zero in the predicted direction.

\paragraph{Scope.}
We study two open models, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, on two datasets, real analysis and MATH, over six feedback turns on the criticism, praise and control tracks.
On MATH we add stance-free tracks that vary the tone of the feedback without saying whether the statement holds (\S\ref{sec:res-stancefree}).
